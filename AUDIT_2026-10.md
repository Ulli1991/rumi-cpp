## Findings

### F1. Fly-by step uses binary component velocities (largest speed effect)
`gravity/n2_kernels.h` `DtK`/`AccDtK`: the pair fly-by time and the symmetrisation terms use the source's own
velocity unless `FLYBY_CM_VELOCITY` (default false, `main.cpp:475`). Binary orbital speeds (tens of km/s) vs
dispersion (few km/s) shorten t_fb = r/v for every field star whose limiting pair is a binary member: ~130k of
200k stars drop 2-3 levels. With c.o.m. velocities (FROST-CLUSTERS II, Sec. 2.1) occupancy equals the
single-star run and the steady step time halves.
Open: energy after 20 steps 1.0e-10 vs 1.3e-11 (single-star run 2.1e-10). 3-seed, 35-step A/B running
(`seeds-cmfb-2026-10-10/`, job 31061441). Also: the CPU path (`integrator_cpu.cpp:419`) and the HHS_CC kernels
still use component velocities.

### F2. Per-call subsystem overhead at deep levels
With F1 on, subsystem integration is 53 % of the step (~3 s): depths 3-7 issue 32-512 calls per step with
1-28 subsystems each (LogH table in the profiler). Time is pack/unpack (device <-> host mirrors) and host
parallel launches rather than integration. Options: serial host path below a batch size; Kepler/secular and
LogH on the device so no copies are needed (plan M7.5).

### F3. LogH retry stalls
Step 9 of the F1 run: 2/1138 subsystems exceeded `ETA_RESTART` = 1e-8; the retry splits dt into up to 256
pieces: 57 s for that step (baseline run: one 10 s retry). Counter shows `cap=12` (subsystems hitting the
1000-GBS-step cap, `logh_batch.cpp:15`). To find: why these subsystems fail (step cap vs GBS
non-convergence vs genuine energy error), and replace brute-force splitting.
Log (`run_b0.1_cm.log` ~line 32590): both subsystems hit the 1000-step cap (`fail=0 overshoot=0`, dE=1e30
sentinel) after reaching 38-44 % of the target time with GBS steps ds ~ 0.02-0.15 (accuracy-limited, not
failing). They need > 2500 regularised steps per call: hierarchical systems (tight inner binary inside a
wider orbit) integrated orbit by orbit. Splitting dt cannot help (every piece needs the same steps per
time). This is the case SDAR's slow-down addresses (plan M3); short-term: raise/adapt the cap or
treat stable hierarchies with SDAR/secular rather than retry-splitting.

### F6. IC binaries merge at their first pericentre
~30 stellar mergers at t = 0.003 Myr with a ~ 1e-7 pc (~4 Rsun) and e = 0.8-0.99 (`[MERGER #556-584]`). The
binary generator (`ic_gen/initial_conditions.cpp:477`) draws thermal e without requiring
r_p = a(1-e) > R1 + R2 (FROST-CLUSTERS II enforces it). Also pairs by mass rank (q ~ 1), unlike BIFROST
App. A / Moe & Di Stefano. Plan M2.

### F4. Secular binaries ~16 us per evolution
~73k secular evolutions per step (F1 on; 220k before) cost ~1.2 s. Expected cost of a Kepler drift is ~1 us.
To check: internal sub-step count (step uses omega/omega-dot, `collisional/secular.cpp:127-140`, -> 0 as
omega -> 0), serial batches below 32 binaries, elements <-> Cartesian conversions.
Per-depth phase table (F1 run): secular takes 0.84 s at depths 3-5 in 224 calls (4-7 ms per call) although
few binaries are active there, while depths 6-7 (768 calls) take 0.1 s. So the cost is internal sub-steps,
not call overhead. Prime suspect: `t_sec = 0.01 * |omega / omega-dot|` collapses for binaries with omega
near 0 (n_steps up to 100000); the precession criterion should use the precession period (2 pi / omega-dot)
or 1/omega-dot, not the current angle.
**Update 2026-10-11:** criterion fixed (1/|omega-dot|; test `secular_omega0_steps`: BH binary rotated to
omega = 1e-6 rad took 109 internal steps per evolution before, 1 after). But for *stellar* binaries the 1PN
precession per step is ~1e-7 rad, so the old term only bites for omega < ~1e-5 -- rare. This does **not**
explain the 5-7 ms secular calls at depths 3-5. A sub-step counter is now printed
(`Secular=N (substeps=M)`); measure on the next cluster run before guessing again. PN secular terms are also applied to all stellar binaries
(`g_collisional.pn_order` passed for every binary), harmless but costly. LogH pack/unpack at depth 7:
~0.6 ms per call (per-call overhead, F2).

### F5. Secular-triple driver ignores the identified hierarchy (accuracy)
`integrator.cpp:646-660`: the inner pair is taken as `particle_ids[0..1]` (pid order) instead of
`inner_binary_id` / `outer_companion_pid` used by the classification (`subsystem.cpp:500-545`). Prime
suspect for the 2.8e-9 late-step regression (viper, steps >= 27). Fix test-first (plan M1.1).
**Worse than scrambling (found with the test):** with the outer body at the lowest id the "inner pair" is
unbound, and the driver hit `if (oe_in.a <= 0 || oe_out.a <= 0) continue;`: the triple was skipped for the
whole step. The FSI drifts no subsystem members, so the three stars (internal orbits and c.o.m.) stayed
frozen. Energy unchanged -> invisible to the energy check and restarts. ~2/3 of secular triples in clusters.
Fixed 2026-10-10: hierarchy from `inner_binary_id` / `outer_companion_pid`; any triple the secular path
cannot evolve goes to LogH instead of being skipped; triple tracker picks the inner pair by distance.
Test `triple_id_order` (fails before: 0 secular calls, |dE| = 0; passes after: identical runs).
Also: the secular-triple path itself loses |dE/E| = 3.4e-5 in 10 steps on this isolated triple (phase
treatment of the orbit average) -- far above the 1e-10 targets; argues for SDAR (plan M3).
General lesson: a subsystem path that `continue`s freezes its members silently. Add an invariant check
(every eligible subsystem advanced exactly once per call) as a debug/test mode.
